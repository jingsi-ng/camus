Why I Chose Camus

"In the depth of winter, I finally learned that within me there lay an invincible summer."

— Return to Tipasa

Source: Wikimedia Commons

How I Found Camus

I remember it was a very confusing time for me. I was asking myself questions like "what is the point of all this?" and "why should I keep trying if everything ends the same way?" These questions kept coming back, and I could not find any answers that felt true to me.

Then I read The Myth of Sisyphus. The image of Sisyphus pushing his boulder up the mountain, watching it roll back down, and then walking down to push it again, it stayed in my mind for a long time. Camus wrote that "the struggle itself towards the heights is enough to fill a man's heart." I think this is when I started to understand something important.

"Life has no meaning. But we can give it meaning."

Reading His Books

Many of his characters look cold and indifferent on the surface. But actually what Camus wants to express is something about the desire for life. When you finish reading his books, you feel full of vitality inside.

In The Stranger, Meursault does not play the social game. He refuses to lie. In the courtroom, he just watches quietly as everyone judges his soul, but nobody gives him a chance to speak. He is always an outsider, standing at the edge of society.

In A Happy Death, I found this idea: maybe the only task for humans is to make ourselves happy. We should pursue happiness without counting the cost. This sounds simple but it is actually quite difficult to do.

"In the human heart, there are more things worthy of praise than things that deserve contempt.."

What He Taught Me

Every time I feel empty or lost, I go back to read Camus. His writing does not give me easy answers or tell me everything will be okay. Instead, he tells me: yes, life might be meaningless. But so what? Keep living anyway.

In The Plague, Camus says that in the human heart, there are more things worthy of praise than things that deserve contempt. I think this is a beautiful way to look at the world. Even when facing absurdity, even when things are difficult, we can still choose to see the good in people.

Why This Website

I built this website because I wanted to share what I learned from Camus. He is not perfect, and I do not want to make him into some kind of saint. He was just a person, with his own problems and mistakes. But he asked the same questions that I ask myself, and his answers helped me a lot.

If you are also looking for something, maybe not meaning exactly, but just a way to keep going, I hope this website can help you find Camus too.
