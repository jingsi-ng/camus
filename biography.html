Biography

1913 - 1660 · From Algerian poverty to Nobel Prize

Life Timeline

1913
Born in Algeria

Albert Camus was born on November 7, 1913, in Mondovi, Algeria (then French Algeria)[1]. His fatehr died in World War 1 when ALbert was less than 1 year old. He grew up in poverty with his mother, grandmother and brother in Algiers [2].

1930
University Years

Attended the University of Algiers, studying philosophy[3]. Diagnosed with tuberculosis at age 17, which would affect him throughout his life [1]. His teacher, Jean Grenier, bacame a major influence on his philosophical development [3].

The Stranger Published

Published both The Stranger (novel) and The Myth of Sisyphus (philosophical essay) in the same year[4]. These works established him as a major voice in existential literature. The Stranger's famous opening -- "Mother died today" -- became iconic [4].

1943
French Resistance

Joined the French Resistance during World War 2, editing the underground newspaper Combat[2]. His wartime experiences deeply influenced his philosophy and his essay Letters to a German friend [1].

1947
The Plague

Published The Plague, an allegorical novel set in Algeria about a plague epidemic[5]. The novel explores themes of solidarity, resistance and human responsibility in the face of catastrophe [6].

1957
Nobel Prize

At age 44, became the second youngest recipient of the Nobel Prize in Literature[7]. The Swedish Academy honored him "for his important Literary production, which with clear-sighted earnestness illuminates the problems of the human conscience in our times" [7].

1960
Untimately Death

Died in a car accident on January 4, 1960, at age 46[2]. An unused train ticket was found in his pocket - he had planned to take the train instead. His unfinished novel The First Man was found in the wreckage and published posthumously in 1994 [1].

Early Life

Born into poverty in French Algeria, Camus never knew his father who died in World War 1[1]. Despite hardship, he excelled academically with help from teachers who recognised his talent[3].

Philosophy

Though often associated with existentialism, Camus rejected the label[3]. His philosophy of the absurd argued that life has no inherent meaning, but we must embrace it anyway[8].

Political Stance

Active in left-wing politics, Resistance fighter during World War 2[2], but broke with Sartre over Soviet comunism[3]. Advocated for Algerian independence but struggled with the violence involved[1].

Personal Life

Married twice, had two children[2]. Lifelong battle with tuberculosis[1]. Passionate about football (soccer), theatre and the Mediterranean sun. Died tragically at 46[2].
